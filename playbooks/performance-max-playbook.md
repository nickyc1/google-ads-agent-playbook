# Performance Max Playbook

Performance Max can work very well when the account gives it good inputs.

It can also hide where the money is going if the feed, measurement, and
business rules are weak.

PMax amplifies the system it is given.

That is the whole playbook.

Do not treat PMax like a magic campaign type. Treat it like a distribution
system that needs clean product data, clean conversion data, clear business
rules, and enough creative to understand what it should be selling.

## PMax Is Best When

- conversion tracking is clean
- product data is complete
- Merchant Center is healthy
- the account has enough conversion volume
- asset coverage is strong
- audiences are useful signals
- landing pages match product and search intent
- the business can define what good revenue means

## PMax Is Risky When

- purchase tracking is broken or duplicated
- all revenue is treated equally despite margin differences
- brand demand is mixed with prospecting
- returning customers are counted as new growth
- product feeds are incomplete
- out-of-stock or low-margin products are eligible
- assets are thin or generic
- final URL expansion is sending traffic to weak pages

## Asset Groups

Asset groups should have a clear business role.

Useful organizing principles:

- product family
- margin tier
- customer use case
- seasonality
- promo
- funnel stage
- new customer acquisition versus retention

Avoid asset groups that are different only because someone needed a naming
convention.

Each asset group should answer:

- What customer or use case is this for?
- What product set is eligible?
- What margin or business role does this product set have?
- What creative angle is being tested?
- What landing pages are allowed?
- What audience signals help the system start in the right place?

If the answer is "all products to all people," the asset group is usually too
blunt.

## Listing Groups

Use listing groups to control which products are eligible.

For ecommerce, this is one of the most important PMax controls.

Check:

- eligible products
- excluded products
- custom labels
- margin tiers
- inventory health
- product families
- test status

Recommended listing group splits:

| Split | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Product family | Separates different customer intent and creative angles |
| Margin tier | Prevents low-margin revenue from looking better than it is |
| Sales tier | Separates heroes, proven products, tests, weak products, and new products |
| Campaign role | Separates acquisition products, AOV builders, seasonal products, and exclusions |
| Inventory status | Prevents limited inventory or stale products from distorting learning |

If listing groups are weak, fix Merchant Center labels before rebuilding PMax.

## Brand And Existing Customer Leakage

PMax may capture demand that would have happened anyway.

Separate:

- brand
- non-brand
- returning customers
- new customers
- remarketing
- prospecting

Use brand exclusions, new-customer settings, customer lists, and business
reporting to evaluate quality.

Ask the agent to separate:

```text
What did PMax create that would not have happened anyway?
```

That is the question.

Not:

```text
What ROAS does Google Ads show?
```

In-platform ROAS can be useful, but it is not the business truth by itself.

## Final URL Expansion

Final URL expansion can help PMax find relevant pages.

It can also send traffic to pages that should not be part of the campaign.

Before enabling or scaling it, review:

- pages PMax is allowed to use
- pages that should be excluded
- whether product pages match the feed
- whether category pages are strong enough
- whether blog or content pages should be excluded
- whether search intent matches the destination page

Use page feeds or URL exclusions when the account needs tighter control.

## Creative Inputs

PMax needs more than the feed.

Review:

- headlines
- long headlines
- descriptions
- images
- logos
- videos
- lifestyle assets
- founder or creator videos
- product demos
- social proof
- offer messaging

Weak creative can make PMax over-rely on Shopping inventory, brand demand, or
remarketing.

For video, load:

- `playbooks/demand-gen-youtube-playbook.md`
- `playbooks/youtube-creator-partnerships-playbook.md`

## Audience Signals

Audience signals are not hard targeting.

They are clues.

Useful signals:

- customer lists
- high-value customer lists
- cart abandoners
- purchasers
- custom segments based on search terms
- competitor or category intent
- site visitors by product family
- YouTube engaged audiences

Bad signals are often worse than no signals because they teach the system the
wrong starting point.

## New Customer Acquisition

Use new customer acquisition settings carefully.

Before judging NCA performance, confirm:

- customer lists are fresh
- repeat customers are being identified correctly
- purchase conversion values are clean
- new customer value rules make business sense
- CRM or first-party reporting agrees with platform directionally

Do not let PMax call returning-customer revenue "growth" because the exclusion
logic is weak.

## Search Terms And Insights

PMax search term visibility is improving, but it is still not the same as a
Search campaign.

Use available search terms, search categories, landing pages, asset group
signals, and product-level performance to infer what PMax is doing.

The agent should look for:

- brand-heavy demand
- irrelevant categories
- product families getting spend they should not receive
- products with clicks but poor conversion quality
- products with good economics but low traffic
- search themes that should become Search campaigns

## Budget Changes

Do not scale PMax only because the campaign-level ROAS looks good.

Before increasing budget, check:

1. Is spend going to products the business wants to scale?
2. Is growth coming from new customers or existing demand?
3. Are low-margin products distorting revenue?
4. Are feed issues holding back better products?
5. Are creative assets strong enough for more reach?
6. Are recent results stable or just one promo window?
7. What is the rollback if performance drops?

## Weekly PMax Review

1. Check Merchant Center feed health.
2. Review listing group eligibility and exclusions.
3. Review product-level spend, revenue, and margin.
4. Review new versus returning customer mix.
5. Review search categories or search terms where available.
6. Review landing pages receiving traffic.
7. Review asset performance and missing creative types.
8. Review budget pacing against business goals.
9. Decide whether to scale, hold, restructure, or fix inputs.

## Agent Rule

Before recommending a PMax budget increase, explain whether the growth is
coming from new demand, branded demand, returning customers, or product mix.
