# Demand Gen Creative Acquisition System

Reviewed: 2026-10-08 (America/Chicago)

Execution companion to the [Demand Gen and YouTube overview](../demand-gen-youtube-playbook.md).
Purpose: test whether proven paid-social creative can help acquire additional
customers through Google's visual inventory at acceptable economics.

Search and PMax can acquire customers too. A campaign type does not establish
whether demand was created, whether a buyer is new, or whether a sale was
incremental. Likewise, Demand Gen can capture warm demand. State its intended
job and verify the audience mix and outcomes.

## Entry Criteria

- Evidence of purchase-producing paid-social concepts, not only high views,
  an attractive ROAS screenshot, or many ads in an ad library.
- Products with viable contribution margin, inventory, and credible pages.
- Deduplicated purchase tracking, reliable values, and first-time customer
  definitions reconciled with the commerce system.
- Permission to use and edit footage, music, testimonials, and likenesses on
  the planned placements, plus an owner who can produce new iterations.
- An approved learning budget, loss limit, decision date, and reporting plan.

## Three Creative Tracks

| Track | Starting material | Work to do | Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Adapt proven creative | Actual paid-social purchase winners | Rework opening, framing, captions, pacing, thumbnail and CTA for the selected Google format | Does the existing concept transfer? |
| Rebuild winning angles | Reasons the winner worked: problem, proof, persona, offer | New creator/demo/stitch for YouTube; separate image/headline concept for Discover | Does the same motivation work in a native execution? |
| Explore new concepts | Customer research and unmet objections | Original animation, demonstration, interview, or carefully reviewed AI-assisted assets | Can a new explanation outperform the current approach? |

Start with adaptation and angle rebuilds. Reserve a small, explicitly capped
part of the learning budget for unfamiliar formats. Neither AI nor animation
is the strategy by itself. Use original, appropriately licensed work and
truthful product depictions; do not fabricate testimonials or affiliation.

Count concepts separately from hooks, aspect ratios, and cutdowns. A large
asset count is not evidence of learning. Only activate as many variants as
the test budget can give a meaningful chance to perform.

## Placement And Page Fit

| Surface | Creative decision | Destination hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube in-stream | Opening earns attention; explain/demo/prove; explicit next action | Product page for clear offers; educational page if explanation is needed |
| YouTube Shorts | Vertical, legible, quickly understandable; intentional recut | Mobile-first page carrying the same product promise |
| YouTube in-feed | Thumbnail and headline set an honest expectation | Match the selected ad format and onward buying journey |
| Discover | Purpose-built image/headline pair, not an arbitrary video still | Product, comparison, or guide appropriate to the promise |

Channel controls are at ad-group level; reporting can separate channels and
YouTube formats. A YouTube-focused hypothesis can use explicit channel
selection. Demand Gen's total spend is not automatically YouTube spend.
Google recommends all channels for general optimization; manual selection
here is an experimental choice with a reach/efficiency tradeoff.
[Channel controls and reporting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15973205?hl=en).

## Campaign Discipline

Keep the existing profitable acquisition/capture foundation stable while
testing. Start with one focused campaign and only enough ad groups to express
the real hypotheses. Use a separately budgeted campaign only when independent
spend control is necessary; ad groups do not guarantee equal allocation.

Keep acquisition and intentional retention hypotheses separate. Use eligible,
consented purchaser lists and available exclusion/lifecycle settings aligned
to the campaign's job. Verify the available mode in the account before launch.
Lists and automated detection cannot identify every existing customer. Google
supports customer lifecycle goals across multiple campaign types, not just
Demand Gen. [Customer lists and lifecycle goals](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14007601?hl=en).

Do not equate automated creative allocation with a randomized A/B test.
Use a supported experiment when causal creative or campaign comparison is
the objective; label normal ad rotations as directional learning.

## Test Brief And Decision Rules

Before launch, record:

- Product, buyer situation, concept, source evidence, offer, and destination.
- Channel/format, audience settings, buyer exclusions, and known overlap.
- Purchase objective, attribution windows, expected conversion lag, baseline,
  and the primary business metric.
- Target acquisition economics, total test budget, per-concept exposure plan,
  end date, owner, stop-loss, approval, and rollback.

Example operating cadence: brief two distinct concepts with two hooks each,
QA rights and destination, then run only the subset affordable at the account's
expected acquisition cost. This is a planning example, not a required cadence.

Use mature conversion cohorts. Tracking failures and approval problems require
immediate diagnosis; they do not establish that the creative failed. Stop at
the agreed loss limit even if the result remains inconclusive. A multiple-of-
CAC stop-loss controls downside; it does not prove statistical significance.

Scale when purchase economics, customer quality, inventory, and broader
business results support it. High CTR, cheaper views, or a higher spend ceiling
alone are not promotion criteria. Log budget changes and avoid simultaneous
major changes to bids, audiences, creative, and pages.

## Measurement Stack

1. Platform: purchases, value, CPA/ROAS, channel and format, conversion lag,
   and click/engaged-view/view-through distinctions where reported.
2. Commerce: actual first-time orders, net revenue, refunds, contribution,
   and blended acquisition efficiency. Total store new customers are not all
   attributable to the campaign.
3. Survey: first-heard channel counts, answer denominator, response coverage,
   and wording consistency. Organic and paid YouTube can overlap in recall.
4. Incrementality: a powered holdout/lift or geographic design when feasible;
   otherwise clearly label directional evidence and its confounders.

Never add platform, survey, and analytics conversions together as separate
customers. Survey share is not automatically incremental revenue share.
Specify whether a blended ratio is spend/revenue or revenue/spend.

## Practical Ramp

| Phase | Work | Gate to continue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | Audit tracking, economics, rights, winning concepts, and pages | Usable evidence and agreed loss limit |
| Initial test | Launch a concentrated adaptation/angle test | Delivery and tracking healthy; risk within cap |
| Iteration | Diagnose hook, promise, proof, page and customer quality | Purchase evidence justifies another iteration |
| Expansion | Add a new concept or surface, then test higher spend | Mature economics and capacity support expansion |

A 90-day engagement can contain these phases with monthly reviews. It is not
permission to spend for 90 days regardless of results. No universal creative
count, channel allocation, or media budget guarantees success.
