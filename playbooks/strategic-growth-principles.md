# Strategic Growth Principles

Last updated: 2026-07-01

These are high-level Google Ads principles for ecommerce accounts. They are
written as operating rules for an AI agent or human operator, not as a list of
guaranteed tactics.

## 1. Separate Capture From Creation

Brand Search, branded Shopping, and some remarketing capture demand that already
exists. PMax, Demand Gen, YouTube, non-brand Search, and comparison pages can
create or expand demand.

Do not judge the account only as one blended ROAS number. First ask:

- Which campaigns capture existing demand?
- Which campaigns create or expand demand?
- Which campaigns are broken because tracking, feed, or landing pages are bad?

## 2. Merchant Center Is A Growth Layer

For ecommerce, the feed is not backend plumbing. It tells Google what the
product is, who it is for, what searches it can match, and whether it is
eligible to show.

Feed work comes before advanced optimization:

- live product coverage
- approval and disapproval fixes
- required attributes
- product type and category cleanup
- title and description quality
- custom labels for product control
- stale and out-of-stock cleanup

## 3. Product Economics Should Shape Campaign Structure

Do not let every SKU compete equally. Products with different margins, AOV,
inventory, lifecycle stage, and conversion behavior often need different
treatment.

Use labels before splitting campaigns. Split only when the account has enough
signal and the products need different budgets, targets, or business decisions.

## 4. Query Coverage Is A System

More keyword coverage does not only mean adding Search keywords. Coverage can
come from:

- feed titles
- Merchant Center attributes
- product pages
- collection pages
- comparison pages
- Search campaigns
- PMax signals
- Demand Gen creative and landing pages

The best accounts make the same truthful product language visible across the
feed, ads, and landing page.

## 5. Demand Gen Is A Creative-Led Growth Channel

Demand Gen should not be treated as leftover YouTube inventory. For many
ecommerce brands, it can act as a cold-traffic testing engine when the account
has:

- strong visual products
- clear offers
- enough creative volume
- clean conversion and value signals
- focused audience or intent hypotheses
- placement/channel reporting
- stop-loss rules

Demand Gen tests should answer one question at a time. Broad, mixed tests with
too many ad groups, audiences, and videos usually dilute the learning.

## 6. Creative Is A Control Lever

As platform targeting becomes more automated, the controllable inputs shift
toward:

- product choice
- offer
- landing page
- hook
- creator/persona
- proof
- format coverage
- first-party data
- conversion quality

Winning creative is not just decoration. It teaches the system what kind of
buyer the product should attract.

## 7. Comparison Demand Deserves Its Own Page

Competitor and "best product" searches are high-intent, but generic landing
pages waste that intent. Build pages that explain the decision criteria and
substantiate the claims.

Safer starting points:

- "Best [product category]"
- "Top [product category]"
- "[Product type] comparison"
- "[Brand] vs [competitor]" only when the comparison is accurate and reviewed

## 8. Scale Only After Signal Is Clean

Aggressive budget scaling should come after:

- purchase/value tracking is reliable
- the feed is eligible and accurate
- products are grouped intentionally
- cold and warm audiences are not blended accidentally
- creative has enough data to judge
- the campaign has a defined job
- stop-loss rules are set

The goal is not to avoid tests. The goal is to avoid scaling noise.

## 9. Advanced Feed Tactics Come Last

Duplicate feeds, multiple listings, and aggressive title variants can expand
surface area, but they add operational and policy risk. Try them only after
feed health, measurement, product identity, and rollback paths are reliable.

## 10. Daily Recommendations Need Context

Every recommendation should classify the campaign first:

- Evergreen
- Test
- Broken

Then recommend the action. A bad test should not cause unnecessary cuts to the
evergreen engine, and a good branded day should not hide weak acquisition.
