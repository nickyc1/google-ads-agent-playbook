# Google Shopping Playbook

Shopping campaigns are built on product data, not keywords.

The feed decides what can show. Product groups decide what the campaign can
control. Measurement decides whether the account is learning the right thing.

## When To Use Standard Shopping

Use Standard Shopping when you need:

- more product-level control
- cleaner Shopping tests
- feed and title experiments
- margin or product-family segmentation
- campaign priority controls
- a clearer view than PMax provides

## Product Group Basics

Product groups are how Standard Shopping campaigns include, subdivide, bid on,
or exclude inventory.

Common subdivisions:

- item ID
- brand
- product type
- Google product category
- condition
- channel
- custom labels

If the feed does not contain useful attributes, the campaign cannot segment
usefully.

## Campaign Structure

Useful Shopping structures:

- hero products
- margin tiers
- product families
- bestsellers
- test products
- seasonal products
- clearance or low-margin products
- branded versus non-brand demand when query control is possible

Do not build a structure the account cannot maintain.

## Shopping Checks

Before changing bids or budgets:

- Is the product approved in Merchant Center?
- Is it in stock?
- Is it included in an active product group?
- Is it excluded at a parent or child level?
- Is an inventory filter blocking it?
- Did the feed taxonomy or item ID change?
- Do custom labels support the control we want?

## What To Test

- product title order
- product image style
- product group segmentation
- bids by margin tier
- product exclusions
- landing-page message match
- Merchant Center promotions

## Agent Rule

Do not call Shopping performance a bidding problem until you have checked feed
quality, product eligibility, labels, landing-page match, and measurement.
